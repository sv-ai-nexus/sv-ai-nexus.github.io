# Talk Details

## Talk 1 &ndash; Securing the Agentic Era: How AI Is Rewriting the Rules of Attack and Defense {#talk-1}

**Speaker**
&ndash;
**<a target="_blank" href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/silverdel/">Min Pyo Hong</a>** - Founder &amp; CEO of SEWORKS

<span class="emph">AI has become a weapon, a target, and a shield, all at the same time.</span>

On the offensive side, AI now writes convincing phishing campaigns in any language, finds vulnerabilities faster than human researchers, and automates attacks that once required skilled teams.
At the same time, AI systems themselves have become targets.
Large Language Models (LLMs) can be manipulated through prompt injection, training data can be poisoned, model weights can be stolen, and open-weight models can be modified and redistributed without their safeguards.

AI agents raise the stakes further.
An agent that can read your email, browse the web, call APIs, and execute code on your behalf is extraordinarily useful, and extraordinarily dangerous when a single malicious web page or document can hijack it.
The security model built for the web and the cloud was not designed for software that takes actions on its own.

This talk looks at AI security from both sides of the fight:

- How attackers are using AI today, and what has genuinely changed versus what is hype.
- The new attack surface of LLMs and agents: prompt injection, agent hijacking, data poisoning, and model theft.
- Why open-weight models and the AI supply chain create security questions that no single company can answer alone.
- How defenders are fighting back with AI-driven detection, automated red-teaming, and continuous security testing.
- What every organization building or deploying AI should be doing right now.

**Why This Matters to AI Nexus**

Almost everyone in our community is building with AI, deploying AI, or investing in AI.
Very few of us are thinking hard enough about how those systems can be attacked.
As agents move from demos into production, security stops being a specialist's concern and becomes every builder's problem.
This talk also sets up the second talk: security is one of the places where the competition between companies and nations becomes most concrete.

## Talk 2 &ndash; AI and the New Power Dynamics {#talk-2}

**Speaker**
&ndash;
**Kyeongrae Cho** - Consul for AI, Consulate General of the Republic of Korea in San Francisco / Ministry of Science and ICT

<span class="emph">AI is no longer simply a technology competition.</span>
It is driving change across industry, the economy, national security, and the international order.

Behind every headline about a new model or a new chip is a deeper contest over who controls the foundations of the AI era.
Open-weight versus closed models, AI safety and regulation, semiconductor supply chains, the energy required to power data centers, and the rise of AI agents are not isolated issues.
Together they define the interests, alliances, and rivalries of Big Tech companies and nations.

This talk will explore:

- How the competition among Big Tech companies and among nations is being reshaped by AI.
- Key flashpoints: open weights, AI safety, semiconductors, energy, and AI agents.
- How the interests of companies and governments align, and where they collide.
- Korea's strengths in this changing global AI landscape.
- The strategic choices Korea faces in the years ahead.

**Why This Matters to AI Nexus**

Silicon Valley sees the AI race from the inside of its companies.
Seoul sees it from the perspective of national strategy.
Consul Cho, who serves at the Consulate General as Consul for AI on behalf of the Ministry of Science and ICT, stands at exactly the point where these two views meet.
For Korean founders, engineers, researchers, and investors in Silicon Valley, understanding the power dynamics behind AI is no longer optional. It shapes which technologies get built, which markets open up, and which partnerships matter.

# Why This Forum Matters

AI is advancing faster than our ability to secure it or govern it.

On one side, AI systems are becoming more capable, more autonomous, and more deeply embedded in businesses and governments.
Agents are starting to act on our behalf.
Open-weight models are spreading capabilities worldwide within days of release.
On the other side, the foundations of AI, including chips, energy, data, and talent, are concentrated in the hands of a small number of companies and countries, and those actors are competing hard.

Three questions define this moment:

1. **Who is attacking, and how?**
   AI is lowering the cost of cyberattacks while creating entirely new categories of vulnerability. Understanding the threat is the first step toward building AI systems that deserve our trust.

1. **Who holds power in the AI era?**
   The competition over models, semiconductors, and energy is reshaping relationships between Big Tech and governments, and between allies and rivals.

1. **Where does Korea stand, and what should it choose?**
   Korea has world-class strengths in semiconductors, manufacturing, and digital infrastructure. The question is how to turn those strengths into strategic advantage amid a rapidly shifting global landscape.

[Oct-2026 AI Nexus Forum](#top) will explore these questions through two talks, a lightning talk, a panel discussion with questions from the floor, and an evening of conversation over dinner.
